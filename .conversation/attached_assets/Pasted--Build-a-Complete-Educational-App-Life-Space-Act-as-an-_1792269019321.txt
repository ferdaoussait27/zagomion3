# Build a Complete Educational App: Life & Space

Act as an expert UI/UX designer, full-stack developer, children's educator, and space-science specialist.

Build a complete, functional, interactive educational web app called **Life & Space**, designed for children aged 7–11 and inspired by NASA science and open data.

The app must teach children how astronauts survive in space and how we can protect life on Earth. The main experience should be a step-by-step simulation of an astronaut's life aboard a space station.

Do not create just a landing page or a static prototype. Build the actual working app with interactive lessons, validation, exercises, progress tracking, and responsive layouts.

## 1. Main concept

The child becomes the mission commander and helps a cute astronaut named Nova complete daily activities in space.

Nova needs to:
- Breathe safely and maintain healthy cabin air.
- Eat suitable food and stay hydrated.
- Recover and conserve water.
- Maintain personal hygiene.
- Grow plants and understand their role in life-support research.
- Manage waste and recycle materials where appropriate.
- Understand exercise, sleep, and daily routines in microgravity.
- Learn how astronauts protect life in space and how people can protect Earth's ecosystems.

The simulation should feel like an interactive space mission, not a collection of ordinary quizzes.

## 2. The most important feature: Step-by-step astronaut simulation

Create a guided mission with clearly numbered steps.

**Never automatically advance to the next step.**

Each step must follow this exact sequence:

1. Explain the current situation in simple, child-friendly English.
2. Tell the child what Nova needs to do and why.
3. Display an interactive activity that the child must complete.
4. Provide a button labeled **Validate Step**.
5. Check the child's answer or action.
6. If the answer is incorrect or incomplete, explain the mistake kindly and let the child try again.
7. If the answer is correct, show a success message explaining the science behind it.
8. Only then enable a button labeled **Continue to Next Step**.
9. Advance to the next step only when the child clicks that button.

Validation must be real. Do not display a success message simply because the child clicks Validate Step.

Do not use fake validation, automatic progression, or buttons that do nothing.

### Example interaction

**Step 2: What Should Nova Eat?**

Nova is preparing a meal aboard the International Space Station.

Display several illustrated food choices, such as:
- A suitable packaged space meal.
- A fruit or vegetable option presented in an appropriate space-food context.
- A drink pouch.
- An unsuitable loose food item that could create floating crumbs.

Ask the child to select a suitable meal and explain why food packaging and crumbs matter in microgravity.

The child selects their answer and clicks **Validate Step**.

If the answer is correct:
- Display: “Excellent! You helped Nova choose a suitable space meal.”
- Explain that astronauts use specially prepared foods and carefully designed packaging because crumbs and floating liquids can cause problems.
- Enable **Continue to Next Step**.

If the answer is incorrect:
- Display a helpful hint.
- Explain the relevant concept without shaming the child.
- Allow another attempt.
- Keep the Continue button disabled until the answer is correct.

Make every step follow this same interaction pattern.

## 3. The complete astronaut mission

Create at least these 10 sequential steps:

### Step 1 — Welcome to the Space Station
Introduce Nova, explain the mission, and teach what makes living in space different from living on Earth.

Activity: Help Nova identify the space station and the basic resources humans need to survive.

### Step 2 — What Should Nova Eat?
Teach how astronauts eat specially prepared food in microgravity.

Activity: Choose suitable food and packaging, then explain why loose crumbs can be a problem.

### Step 3 — Staying Hydrated
Explain why water is essential and how astronauts use water responsibly.

Activity: Make sensible water-use choices and identify which actions reduce waste.

### Step 4 — Breathing in Space
Explain that astronauts need a controlled supply of breathable air.

Activity: Match oxygen supply, carbon dioxide removal, and cabin-air monitoring to their functions.

Do not suggest that astronauts can breathe ordinary unprocessed spacecraft air without life-support systems.

### Step 5 — Recycling Water
Introduce NASA's Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS).

Activity: Arrange a simplified water-recovery process in the correct order.

Explain that actual spacecraft water treatment is more complex than the simplified activity.

### Step 6 — Personal Hygiene
Explain why astronauts need appropriate hygiene routines in microgravity.

Activity: Help Nova choose suitable hygiene supplies and conserve water.
